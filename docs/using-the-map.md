# Using the map

The screen has three main parts:

- **The map**, filling the window
- **The sidebar** on the left, with search, filters, and the trail list
- **The trail card** on the right, which slides in when you pick a trail

## Moving around

| To… | Mouse | Keyboard / buttons |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Pan | Drag | |
| Zoom | Scroll (zooms toward the cursor) | `+` / `−`, or the **+** / **−** buttons |
| Spin the view | Right-drag | `Q` / `E` rotate 90°, or the ⟲ ⟳ buttons |
| Go back to the whole basin | | `H`, or the ⌂ button |

The compass above the buttons always points north, which helps after you've spun the view.

## Finding a trail

There are three ways to find a trail:

- **Point at it.** Hover over any line on the map to see its name, length, and who can use it. Click to select it.
- **Search.** Press `/` (or click the search box) and start typing. Results update as you type. Use the arrow keys to move through them and `Enter` to pick one.
- **Browse the list.** The sidebar lists every trail alphabetically, with its length. Hovering a list item highlights that trail on the map.

When you select a trail, the map flies to it and highlights it in gold. Everything else fades back a little. Press `Esc`, click an empty spot on the map, or close the card to deselect.

### Filters

The **All / Hike / Bike** buttons narrow both the map and the list:

- **Hike**: trails open to people on foot
- **Bike**: trails open to bikes, including trails where bikes are allowed on part of the route

## Reading a trail card

The card shows the following:

- **Name**, and a location hint (like "near Tahoe City") when several trails share a name
- **Official trail** stamp, if the trail is in the Forest Service's trail inventory
- **Who it's for**: hiking, mountain bikes, or no bikes. Wilderness trails say so. If bike access was inferred rather than stated in the data, the card says "likely allowed".
- **Miles, climbing, and descending**
- **An elevation profile** of the longest continuous stretch
- **Details**, when known: bike difficulty, hike difficulty, surface, who manages it, elevation range, and data source

**Zoom to trail** re-frames the map on the selected trail.

## Sharing a trail

Selecting a trail updates the page's address, for example `…/#trail=Corral%20Trail`. Copy the address and anyone who opens it lands on that trail. Planned trails use `#plan=…` instead (see [The 2026 trail plan](trail-plan.md)).
