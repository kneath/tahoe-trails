# The 2026 trail plan

In January 2026 the U.S. Forest Service approved the **Basin Wide Trails Analysis**, a plan for the Lake Tahoe Basin's national forest land. The plan:

- adds about 45 miles of new trail
- opens about 111 miles of existing trail to Class 1 e-bikes
- builds three new trailheads
- retires a few short, problem trails

The **✦ 2026 trail plan** overlay paints these changes onto the map, so you can see where the network is headed.

## Turning it on

Flip the **2026 trail plan** switch in the sidebar. When it's on:

- Today's trails fade back so the planned changes stand out.
- The legend at the bottom right switches to the plan's colors.
- The trail list shows a **Planned** section above **On the ground today**.

Three toggles under the switch choose what to show:

- **New trails** (on by default): trails to be built, plus existing user-made trails being adopted into the official system
- **E-bike changes** (off by default): existing trails that are opening to Class 1 e-bikes, or to motorcycles in a few places. These cover a lot of the map, so they start hidden.
- **Removals** (on by default): trails to be decommissioned and restored

## Reading the overlay

Trails that aren't built yet are drawn as **long dashes over a soft wash of color**. The color says who they're for:

| Look | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Raspberry dashes | New trail, open to hikers, bikes, and Class 1 e-bikes |
| Indigo dashes | New non-motorized trail (hikers and bikes, no e-bikes) |
| Amber dashes | New motorcycle trail |
| Solid teal | Existing trail, newly open to e-bikes |
| Grey dots | Trail to be removed |
| **P** marker | New trailhead |

Click any planned trail, or pick it from the list or search, to open its card. The card shows:

- an **Approved plan · 2026** stamp, and **not built yet** for new construction
- who the trail will be open to
- its length, climbing, and elevation profile
- the change being made, and the season it's open to e-bikes (April 1 – November 15) or motorcycles (May 25 – November 15)
- the Forest Service's own short note on why the trail is proposed

## Things to keep in mind

- **Routes may move.** These are planning-level lines from the project's maps. Built routes can shift during design.
- **Built in phases.** Construction started in 2026 and will be phased over several years, so "approved" doesn't mean "open".
- **Only the approved plan.** One trail from the original proposal (Angora to N Upper Trails, "S59") was excluded from the final decision, so it isn't shown.
- **Forest Service land only.** The plan covers national forest land. State parks and other land managers aren't part of it.

Learn more on the [project page](https://www.fs.usda.gov/r05/laketahoebasin/projects/54566).
